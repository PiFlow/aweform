# Zhai & Tolley (2026) — desktop FFF soft robots: materials, printers, and Aweform relevance

**Status:** Non-authorizing external research and purchasing reference, recorded 23 November 2026 (Australia/Brisbane). **Not an ADR, D/EXP result, experimental authorization, hardware-selection decision, migration proposal, or roadmap commitment.** No Aweform runtime, architecture, seeds, safety, or learning changes are proposed by this note.

**Primary research:** Yichen Zhai and Michael T. Tolley, *Printing powerful soft robots on a desktop*, *Science Robotics* 11(120), eaei1264 (18 November 2026), [DOI: 10.1126/scirobotics.aei1264](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/scirobotics.aei1264). Original authors' accompanying [Dryad dataset and readme](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi%3A10.5061/dryad.sxksn03m0), DOI: 10.5061/dryad.sxksn03m0. [UC San Diego announcement](https://today.ucsd.edu/story/the-fastest-soft-robot-ever-built-doesnt-need-electronics-to-function). [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43050495/).

## Evidence and reproduction limits

- **[PRIMARY — ARTICLE ABSTRACT]** The authors demonstrate a monolithically 3D-printed, six-legged soft running robot with an integrated four-phase, double-expansion pneumatic oscillator. They report a peak 4.72 m/s speed and estimated 169 W mechanical power and demonstrate outdoor and aquatic movement. Those are author-reported results, not independently reproduced results or a continuous autonomy metric.
- **[PRIMARY — AUTHORS' DRYAD METHODS]** The 2026 devices were fabricated using a **modified desktop fused-filament-fabrication (FFF/FDM) printer**, **thermoplastic elastomer (TPE) filament**, and **continuous Eulerian print paths** intended to form airtight fluidic chambers and controls. The public data package lists spreadsheet measurements plus four STL files: two oscillator designs and two complete soft robots. It does **not** establish that an unmodified retail printer using an ordinary slicer preset will reproduce functioning, pressure-tight devices.
- **[UNVERIFIED]** The accessible 2026 abstract and Dryad methods do not identify the exact filament manufacturer, grade/Shore hardness, printer make/model, specific modifications, or a ready-to-run, validated G-code/slicer recipe. Do not substitute the earlier paper's settings as if they had been verified for the 2026 design. The paywalled full article/supplement may supply additional information not independently checked here.
- **[RELATED PRIMARY — PRIOR WORK, NOT 2026 SPECIFICATION]** The team's 2025 paper, [*Monolithic Desktop Digital Fabrication of Autonomous Walking Robots*](https://doi.org/10.1002/aisy.202400876), describes soft **80A TPU** (thermoplastic polyurethane, a type of TPE) and reports printing using a **modified Raise3D E2**; a separate Prusa replication used a modified filament feed / E3D Hemera direct-drive printhead. Its authors state that consistently extruding 80A TPU without jamming is the key printer requirement. This supports printer-category feasibility, but not exact 2026 replication.
- **[TERMINOLOGY]** TPE is a family of thermoplastic elastomers. TPU is one type. Softer Shore A grades (e.g., 80A or 85A versus 95A) create greater filament-feeding difficulty. A printer's marketing claim of “TPU support” usually does not prove reliable continuous printing of 80A soft filament or airtight valves.
- **[ENERGY/AUTONOMY BOUNDARY]** The 2026 headline high-speed demonstration used an external compressed-air supply, and the researchers have not demonstrated self-directed learning or energy autonomy. Dryad also describes an additional Fig. S20 *untethered CO2-canister performance* test; that limited recorded test must not be conflated with independent, long-duration energetic autonomy. The university announcement describes compressor tethering as the present operating limitation.

## Final recommendation for Aweform

**[RECOMMENDATION / INFERENCE] Preserve this research note; do not change the current V0.5 differential-drive body, current D-061 experience-driven learning priority, simulator commitments, or accepted ADR boundaries.** The transferable principle is **morphological computation**: a fabricated mechanical/fluidic structure can take over some coordination, compliance or physical safety that would otherwise need electronic control. That can be a useful future Level-1 body design principle under [ADR 0018](../adr/0018-three-layer-developmental-architecture-and-innate-viability-floor.md), but it must not be falsely reported as an acquired Level-2/3 skill, learned cognition, biological metabolism or energetic independence.

**[PURCHASE ADVICE — conditional]** If a printer is wanted for broad Aweform-related making and learning independent of this study, prefer the **Bambu Lab P2S printer-only** as the lower-cost starting point. The **Prusa CORE One+** is a higher-cost repairability/support alternative. **Do not buy the Raise3D E2 solely to duplicate this paper** without verifying the precise 2026 material, printhead modifications, toolpath generation and acceptable prototype outcome. A maker-space or printing-service trial is a lower-commitment first step.

**[FUTURE TEST CANDIDATE — NOT AUTHORIZED]** After Aweform has a reproducible physical-contact simulation and an explicitly approved relevant experimental question, compare fixed-control rigid contact against one carefully specified compliant-body variant: record collision impulse, stall/recovery, action/sensation predictability and energetic consequences. Keep the controller and lawful observations matched for the mechanical comparison. A compliant body could help, do nothing, or introduce spring/hysteresis dynamics that make learning harder. A physics result is not proof of autonomous learning. Any physical hardware would need separate safety review, especially for pressurized chambers/air systems.

**[FABRICATION PRACTICALITY]** Before attempting the robot: ask the authors for the exact 2026 TPE make/hardness, printer/extruder changes, layer/flow/temperature profile, and Eulerian-toolpath generation method; check model size against build volume; dry and calibrate the flexible filament; print a small test chamber rather than an entire robot; and evaluate leakage/rupture conservatively with pressure-rated equipment and shielding. Do not treat the authors' 169 W estimated mechanical output as a power-supply sizing specification.
