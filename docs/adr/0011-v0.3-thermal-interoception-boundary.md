# ADR 0011 — V0.3 Thermal Interoception Boundary for D-002

**Status:** Proposed

**Authorisation state:** Not yet authorized for implementation. This ADR must receive exact-HEAD independent GPT-5.6 Sol and Claude Opus 5 PASS reviews under `AGENTS.md`, followed by Flow-authorized merge, before D-002 thermal implementation may begin.

**Scope:** Narrow durable sensory/information and viability-state boundary for the first D-002 thermal ecology. It supplements ADR 0010 and does not amend historical ADR 0009 or EXP-003 records.

## Context

D-001 showed descriptively that the current localized-charging ecology admits trivial post-contact viability. On development seeds `18141`, `18142`, and `18143`, both a constant docked `WAIT` policy and a constant docked `TURN_LEFT` policy remained in charging contact for the full 1000-transition horizon, never fell below starting energy `5.0`, and ended at maximum energy `10.0`.

D-001 does not show that station acquisition is trivial. It isolates the post-acquisition ecology and shows that, once contact is established, no second regulatory pressure currently requires departure from the charger.

D-002 therefore asks for the smallest coherent additional internal constraint that creates a genuine regulation problem without introducing a learner.

ADR 0010 explicitly does not itself authorize a thermal sensor/channel. This ADR proposes that narrow authorization.

## Intended regulation problem

Before any D-002 development execution, the implemented thermal constants must satisfy three qualitative design conditions stated in advance:

1. **Continuous charging contact is eventually thermally non-viable** from the declared nominal initial thermal state.
2. **Continuous absence from charging contact is eventually energetically non-viable** under the unchanged basal/action energy accounting.
3. **The ecology is not trivially impossible:** a controller-independent arithmetic or simulation sanity check must establish that at least one alternating contact/off-contact schedule can, in principle, remain within both energy and thermal viability bounds for a meaningful horizon.

These conditions define the regulation conflict. They are not permission to tune the world until a preferred controller fails. Once a coherent first parameterization is chosen and D-development begins, simple fixed-controller success is an informative result and must not trigger automatic ecological hardening.

## Decision

### A. Add one thermal internal state

D-002 may add exactly one scalar organism thermal state representing its own internal temperature-like viability variable.

The state is simulator-maintained organism state, distinct from evaluator-only world geometry and diagnostics. It is an engineered thermal viability variable, not a claim of biological body temperature or metabolism.

The first D-002 implementation must use a declared ambient/lower reference and a declared upper thermal failure boundary.

Reaching or exceeding the upper thermal failure boundary terminates that organism lifetime as a genuine viability consequence. The first D-002 thermal model does not require a lower-temperature failure boundary.

### B. Add one organism-visible thermal interoceptive channel

The organism may receive a single normalized interoceptive reading derived only from its own current thermal state and the declared thermal bounds.

The initial implementation should expose the deterministic normalized internal thermal state directly, analogous to existing normalized energy interoception. No evaluator coordinates, true station distance, hidden geometry, future thermal state, experiment labels, or other privileged telemetry may be bundled with this channel.

The thermal signal is subject to ADR 0010 sensory/plasticity closure. Any future plastic mechanism may use it only as an organism-visible interoceptive consequence available at the relevant causal time.

Sensor noise, delayed sensing, multiple thermal channels, gradients, and external ambient-field sensing are not authorized by this ADR.

### C. Charging heat is driven by offered station input, not accepted stored-energy increase

The existing EXP-003 energy store remains capacity-limited and clips at maximum energy. Therefore accepted stored-energy increase can fall to zero while the body remains in charging contact.

To avoid recreating the D-001 degeneracy through energy clipping, the first D-002 thermal model must base charging-generated heat on the **offered station charging input associated with genuine post-action charging contact**, not solely on the realized increase in stored battery energy.

Conceptually, station power may be split between stored energy and dissipation. When the energy store is full, continued offered station input may still generate thermal load even though stored energy no longer increases.

This is a declared physical-model choice, not hidden evaluator feedback and not a reward signal.

### D. Use the smallest cooling model first

The first D-002 model may include deterministic passive cooling toward the declared ambient/lower reference on each transition.

Cooling must not depend on hidden evaluator coordinates, true distance from the charger, coverage, or other privileged world state.

Distance-dependent cooling, station-local ambient gradients, environmental temperature maps, and additional thermal reservoirs are deliberately deferred. They require later justification if D-development reveals a specific ambiguity or failure that earns them.

### E. Preserve the existing energy dynamics in the first thermal slice

The first D-002 implementation must leave the established EXP-003 energy accounting unchanged except for adding the independent thermal viability consequence.

In particular, the first slice does **not** add heat-dependent charge throttling, thermal efficiency modulation, battery-capacity degradation, or a controller-side emergency override.

This avoids coupling several new mechanisms at once and makes the first thermal result interpretable.

### F. Do not add actuator-generated heat in the first thermal slice

The first D-002 implementation does not include separate heat generation from turning or movement.

Charging-generated heat plus passive cooling is sufficient to ask whether a second internal constraint creates a genuine energy/temperature regulation problem. Actuator heat may be reconsidered later only if development identifies a concrete reason to add it.

### G. Causal update timing must be explicit

The implementation must document and test the order of action, post-action charging contact, energy update, thermal input/cooling, thermal viability evaluation, and next observation.

The intended first-order rule is that charging heat is caused by genuine post-action contact on the transition in which station input is offered, matching the existing post-action charging-contact convention.

No future observation or post-hoc evaluator outcome may influence the current thermal update.

### H. D-002 remains development-only and non-learning

After this ADR is accepted and merged, D-002 implementation may proceed in the lightweight development lane provided it stays inside this authorized boundary.

D-002 itself must:

- use legal development seeds only;
- add no learner or plastic state;
- preserve negative and null results;
- make no confirmatory claim;
- reserve no EXP-004 seeds;
- state the chosen thermal constants before the first substantive development run;
- preserve simple fixed-policy success rather than retuning to force learning.

D-003 may then characterize fixed-policy/fixed-parameter sufficiency in the resulting ecology, including a cheap thermostatic shuttle or equivalent non-learning feedback controller using only authorized organism-visible signals and minimal own phase state.

## Not authorized by this ADR

This ADR does not authorize:

- learning or plasticity mechanisms beyond the general permission already established by ADR 0010;
- actuator-generated heat in the first D-002 slice;
- heat-dependent charge throttling or efficiency modulation;
- distance-dependent cooling or thermal maps;
- multiple thermal compartments or additional internal viability variables;
- thermal sensor noise, delay, gradients, or external temperature sensing;
- hidden controller rescue logic;
- changes to EXP-003 historical evidence or frozen seed reservations;
- any evidence claim for EXP-004.

## Consequences

- D-002 gains a second internal viability pressure without adding learning.
- Remaining permanently docked is intended to cease being a viable long-run solution because offered charger input carries thermal load even when the energy store is full.
- Remaining permanently off-dock remains energetically non-viable under the unchanged energy rules.
- The resulting world asks for regulation between competing internal constraints rather than merely maximizing time on the charger.
- The first thermal model stays deliberately inspectable: one added state, one added interoceptive channel, one charging-heat path, one passive-cooling path, and one upper thermal failure boundary.
- If a simple fixed feedback controller solves that world, that result is retained as evidence about mechanism sufficiency rather than treated as a design failure.
