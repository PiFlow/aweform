# ADR 0012 — V0.4 Minimal Physical Embodiment, Energy, and Thermal Boundary

**Status:** Proposed while unmerged; **Accepted upon Flow-authorized merge after
the required exact-HEAD GPT-5.6 Sol and Claude Opus 5 PASS reviews**, without
any further content change.

**Authorisation state:** This document does not authorize V0.4 implementation
while unmerged. Once the status condition above is satisfied, this exact
reviewed text becomes the durable V0.4 physical-embodiment, energy, thermal,
and associated information boundary; no post-review status-edit commit is
required.

**Opens:** V0.4. **References, and does not amend or invalidate,** ADRs 0001,
0002, 0008, 0009, 0010, or 0011, or any historical experiment or development
record.

**Evidence basis:** `development/D-019-minimal-v0.4-physical-embodiment-
thermal-budget-audit.md`, inspected with the current repository source and
historical D-002 through D-018 records. D-019 is a development audit, not a
hardware measurement, implementation, or confirmatory result.

## Context

D-019 found that the D-002 thermal model was intentionally artificial and
should not be extended informally. D-002 uses one abstract normalized thermal
state, charging heat based on offered charging input, fixed cooling, the
unchanged abstract EXP-003 energy accounting, and no actuator heat. Those
choices were appropriate for the developmental question then being asked and
remain valid historical semantics.

The next proposed slice is different. It would give the simulator physical
units and a small causal energy/temperature model that could plausibly map to
a future small differential-drive robot. This changes durable architecture,
viability dynamics, physical ecology, and the interpretation of the
organism-visible energy and temperature channels. It therefore requires a
separate accepted boundary before implementation.

The DFRobot miniQ / approximately 122 mm differential-drive platform is used
only as a form-factor and reference-embodiment anchor. D-019 records unresolved
first-party motor information, absent loaded-current and thermal measurements,
and broad engineering uncertainty. The exact commercial chassis is not a
permanent hardware commitment, and no physical robot control is authorized.

This ADR authorizes a deliberately small physicalized simulator ecology. It
does not authorize high-fidelity robotics simulation, a particular learner, or
a claim that the simulator is a biological metabolism or a safe physical
device.

## Historical preservation

This ADR opens a new V0.4 boundary. It does not rewrite history.

In particular:

- D-002 through D-019 retain their existing identifiers, code, records,
  results, and interpretations.
- D-002 intentionally retains one abstract normalized thermal state, heat
  from offered post-action charging input, fixed per-transition cooling,
  unchanged abstract EXP-003 energy accounting, and no actuator heat.
- D-003, D-014, D-018, and the other historical D-series work remain under
  the D-002/EXP-003 semantics that governed them.
- Historical EXP-003 energy accounting and all historical reward, observation,
  action, and seed contracts remain unchanged.
- No historical result becomes a V0.4 result, and no historical controller or
  learned state is assumed to transfer into V0.4.

The physically grounded model authorized below is a new simulator ecology. It
must not be tuned to recreate D-002, D-003, D-014, or D-018 behaviour.

## Decision

### A. Scope and physical reference

After acceptance, V0.4 development may implement the smallest energy/thermal
slice defined in this ADR. The first slice is evaluator physics plus the
existing minimal sensorimotor interface. It should first answer whether the
physical bookkeeping is coherent under simple actions, before asking whether
a learned model can control it.

The reference embodiment is an approximate 122 mm diameter small
differential-drive body. It is a scale and thermal-mass reference only. This
ADR does not commit Aweform to the DFRobot product, its exact motor ratio, its
materials, or a final hardware assembly.

The current point/body-center movement and charging-contact geometry may remain
unchanged for the first V0.4 energy/thermal slice. A geometry change requires
its own developmental justification and, if it changes a durable boundary, an
appropriate reviewed ADR or amendment.

### B. Physical time and nominal movement scale

V0.4 may use the design timestep:

```text
dt = 0.1 seconds per transition
```

This is a provisional simulator design choice supported by D-019, not a
measured hardware truth. Existing actions continue to consume one transition
unless a later explicit boundary changes action timing. Therefore:

```text
1000 transitions = 100 seconds
```

This interpretation applies to V0.4 only and does not alter the interpretation
of any historical run.

The provisional world-scale mapping is:

```text
1 simulator world unit ≈ 1 metre
MOVE_FORWARD = 0.05 simulator units ≈ 5 cm per movement action
```

At `dt = 0.1 s`, the nominal full-step translation is approximately `0.5
m/s`. This is a simulator design choice, not validated motor performance.
D-019 records an unresolved 50:1 versus 75:1 first-party motor ambiguity, no
loaded-speed measurement, and no basis for treating 5 cm/action as a hardware
truth. Future obstacle-rich development may justify a smaller movement
increment. This ADR does not change `MOVE_FORWARD` or the current turn
semantics, and does not introduce wheel dynamics or detailed differential-drive
simulation.

### C. Internal battery energy in joules

V0.4 may replace the abstract internal energy accounting with one scalar
physical battery-energy state in joules. Conceptually:

```text
E_next = clamp(
    E_battery
    + P_stored_charge(charge_phase, SOC) * dt
    - P_electronics_electrical * dt
    - P_actuator_electrical(action) * dt,
    0,
    E_battery_max,
)
```

The first exact `E_battery_max` must be declared before substantive V0.4
development runs. It may be selected within the D-019-supported small-robot
battery range, approximately 4.7–9.6 kJ of nominal usable simulation energy,
but that range is engineering sensitivity territory, not a battery guarantee.
The choice must not be made or tuned to obtain interesting controller
behaviour.

V0.4 retains:

- one scalar internal energy store;
- non-viability/death at depleted energy;
- normalized own-energy organism-visible interoception.

V0.4 does not add battery degradation, ageing, a detailed voltage-versus-SOC
curve, battery-health state, cell-chemistry dynamics, or a voltage observation.

### D. Organism-visible energy

The organism continues to receive only normalized own energy. The conceptual
encoding is:

```text
visible_energy = E_battery / E_battery_max
```

The exact normalization must preserve the existing observation convention
where applicable, and the first V0.4 implementation must document it. The
organism does not receive joules, Wh, voltage, capacity, electrical power,
actuator power, charger efficiency, or an SOC label beyond normalized own
energy.

### E. One absolute own-body temperature

V0.4 may add exactly one lumped own-body temperature state, internally
represented in physical temperature units such as degrees Celsius. This is an
engineered simulator viability state. It is not a claim that the future
assembly is homogeneous, biological, metabolically alive, or literally at one
temperature.

The first V0.4 slice has one effective thermal capacitance `C_thermal` and one
body temperature `T_body`. Multiple thermal compartments are not authorized.

### F. Ambient temperature and information boundary

The evaluator/physics may use a configurable ambient temperature with default:

```text
T_ambient = 23 °C
```

Ambient remains evaluator-only. The organism must not receive ambient
temperature, `T_body - T_ambient`, an external temperature map, charger
temperature, motor temperature, or component temperatures.

Changing ambient may causally change subsequent body temperature through
physical heat exchange. It must not directly change the organism-visible
temperature encoding merely because the evaluator changed ambient. In
particular, the first V0.4 temperature signal must not be an instantaneous
ambient-relative encoding.

### G. Organism-visible temperature

The organism may receive exactly one normalized interoceptive channel derived
from its own current body temperature. The first implementation must declare
fixed physical normalization bounds before substantive runs. Those bounds
must not be tuned from observed behaviour and must not depend on the current
ambient value.

This ADR does not freeze an arbitrary numerical normalization formula where
D-019 does not justify one. It does freeze the requirements that the channel
is:

- one scalar;
- derived from current own `T_body` only;
- ambient-independent in its instantaneous encoding;
- available to later plastic mechanisms only as an actually observed
  organism-visible consequence under ADR 0010.

### H. Passive heat exchange toward ambient

V0.4 replaces D-002's fixed per-transition cooling with signed,
temperature-dependent passive exchange toward ambient. The minimal candidate
is:

```text
P_environment = G * (T_ambient - T_body)
```

where `G` is one effective body/environment thermal conductance. Thus the
environment cools a body hotter than ambient, warms a body colder than
ambient, and exchanges no passive heat at equality. This signed form is the
physically coherent minimal refinement because passive exchange should reverse
direction when the temperature gradient reverses; an arbitrary constant
cooling subtraction cannot represent that symmetry and can create energy from
cooling below the reference without a physical cause.

This is still a one-body lumped approximation. V0.4 does not add environmental
temperature fields, location-dependent cooling, multiple heat reservoirs,
computational fluid dynamics, humidity, or radiation submodels unless a later
reviewed boundary specifically earns one.

D-019 supports approximately `0.15–0.50 W/K` as a broad conductance candidate
range, with low confidence. It is an engineering estimate/sensitivity range,
not measured hardware truth. The first exact `G` must be declared before
substantive runs.

### I. Electronics electrical power and body heat

V0.4 may include a continuous evaluator-side electronics electrical-power term
`P_electronics_electrical`. It must remain distinct from
`P_electronics_body_heat`.

At the first lumped abstraction, most radio-disabled electronics consumption
may be approximated as local body heat. This is an engineering approximation
pending prototype measurement, not a measurement made by D-019. Wi-Fi
transmission is not a baseline heat source; networking remains unauthorized.

D-019's radio-disabled candidate ranges, approximately `0.08–0.20 W` for
idle/light computation and `0.12–0.35 W` for active computation, are
engineering estimates for sensitivity and parameter provenance. They do not
mandate a final value. The first exact electrical and body-heat values must be
declared before substantive runs.

### J. Action-dependent actuator electrical power

V0.4 may include action-dependent evaluator-side actuator electrical power
while retaining the current four-action interface:

```text
WAIT:         no drive-motor electrical load
MOVE_FORWARD: paired-motor electrical load
TURN_LEFT:    differential/in-place-turn electrical load
TURN_RIGHT:   differential/in-place-turn electrical load
```

The first model may use action-class values for `MOVE_FORWARD` and `TURN_LEFT /
TURN_RIGHT`. It must not expose motor state, current, RPM, or power to the
organism. D-019's approximately `0.5–1.5 W` MOVE and `0.3–1.0 W` TURN ranges
are broad engineering estimates pending loaded measurement; stall power is a
peak comparison and must not be used as ordinary locomotion power.

### K. Actuator electrical input and actuator body heat are distinct

This distinction is mandatory:

```text
battery depletion:  P_actuator_electrical(action)
thermal dynamics:   P_actuator_body_heat(action)
```

These terms are not identical. Electrical input can become external mechanical
work, winding loss, gearbox/friction loss, driver loss, and local heat. The
effective fraction conducted into the one-body thermal state is
`UNKNOWN / NEEDS MEASUREMENT`.

ADR 0012 authorizes the structural term `P_actuator_body_heat(action)` but not
the claim that D-019 measured its magnitude. Any first V0.4 engineering
estimate must be:

- explicitly declared for MOVE and TURN before substantive behavioural runs;
- provenance-labelled as an estimate, sensitivity assumption, or unknown;
- fixed for the declared first slice;
- prohibited from being tuned to recreate D-002 thermal behaviour;
- replaceable later by hardware measurement through a reviewed parameter
  update.

Equating all actuator electrical input with body heat is not the default and
requires a separate, explicitly justified sensitivity assumption rather than
being silently embedded in the model.

### L. Charging energy, loss, taper, and termination

V0.4 must not carry forward D-002's rule that continued offered charger input
creates the same thermal load when the battery is full. The physical charging
event must distinguish at least:

- charger/station input power;
- energy accepted and stored by the battery;
- charging-related electrical loss/body heat;
- taper near full state;
- charge termination/full standby behaviour.

A minimal piecewise or otherwise simple SOC-dependent acceptance/taper rule is
authorized. A detailed electrochemical or charger-electronics simulation is
not. The implementation must ensure that when charging is full/terminated,
continued contact does not automatically create the same charging heat as
active mid-SOC charging. Baseline electronics heat may continue while docked.

`P_charging_body_heat` must arise from the charging event and its declared
loss/heat approximation, not from evaluator reward, preferred behaviour, or a
hidden controller objective. Charger phase, accepted charge power, efficiency,
and absolute battery state remain evaluator-only.

D-019 gives switching-charge efficiency and linear-charge-loss values only as
broad engineering references. It does not establish the exact first charger
topology or loss. The first implementation must declare its accepted-power,
body-heat/loss, taper, and termination rules before substantive runs.

### M. Minimal thermal equation

The first V0.4 thermal model may use:

```text
P_body_heat = P_electronics_body_heat
            + P_actuator_body_heat(action)
            + P_charging_body_heat(charge_phase, SOC)

P_environment = G * (T_ambient - T_body)

T_next = T_body + dt * (P_body_heat + P_environment) / C_thermal
```

`C_thermal` is one effective lumped thermal capacitance and `G` is one
effective conductance. D-019 supports approximately `72–285 J/K` as a broad
component envelope and approximately `150–250 J/K` as a narrower candidate
model range, with `180 J/K` only an illustrative center. These are engineering
estimates and sensitivity territory, not final or measured hardware truth.

The first exact `C_thermal` and `G` must be selected and frozen before
substantive V0.4 execution. No parameter may be chosen or iteratively adjusted
to make a thermal conflict interesting.

Movement-dependent cooling is omitted from this first slice. Movement may
generate actuator heat; movement-caused airflow cooling is
`OMITTED / NEEDS MEASUREMENT` until a later result or prototype measurement
earns it.

### N. Temperature viability boundary

V0.4 may use one upper integrated body-temperature viability boundary. D-019
supports an approximate `40–50 °C` candidate range, with approximately `45 °C`
as a provisional design centre. The exact first threshold must be declared
before substantive runs and must represent a conservative integrated operating
boundary, not catastrophic component destruction.

The threshold is a simulator design choice, not a certified hardware safety
limit. It must not be lowered merely to force thermal regulation. No lower-
temperature death boundary is authorized in the first slice without a concrete
new justification and an appropriate boundary review.

### O. Realistic null result is valid

Realistic V0.4 physics may weaken or remove the historical thermal-shuttle
pressure. That is an acceptable result.

Development must not inflate charger losses, equate all actuator electricity
with body heat, lower thermal thresholds, increase electronics heat, alter
cooling, or change battery size merely to recreate D-002/D-003/D-014
behaviour. A simple or absent thermal conflict is evidence about the physical
model. It is not, by itself, evidence that the model failed or that more
complex control is needed.

### P. Deterministic causal transition order

The first implementation must document and test a deterministic transition
order with no future information. At minimum it must be equivalent to:

1. Produce the current organism-visible observation.
2. Select the current action from that observation and already retained,
   closure-valid organism state.
3. Apply physical movement and update physical charging contact.
4. Create the action-dependent electrical-power event.
5. Apply charge acceptance, taper, or termination for the resulting contact
   and current battery state.
6. Integrate battery energy over `dt`.
7. Generate electronics, actuator, and charging body heat for the event.
8. Apply signed passive body/environment heat exchange.
9. Integrate body temperature over `dt`.
10. Evaluate energy and thermal viability.
11. Produce the next organism-visible observation if the transition remains
    observable.

The precise implementation may factor these operations differently, but it
must preserve their causal meaning and test it. In particular, post-action
contact may determine the current transition's charging event, while the
next observation is not available to the current action or update. No
evaluator success label, future observation, future consequence, or reward may
affect the current transition. Reward remains absent/zero unless a separate
future boundary authorizes it.

### Q. Programmed, organism-visible, and evaluator-only separation

The first V0.4 implementation must maintain this separation:

| Category | Authorized contents |
|---|---|
| **Programmed / physics** | `dt`; physical world scale; nominal movement mapping; battery capacity; electrical-power parameters; charge/taper/termination rules; one `C_thermal`; one `G`; evaluator ambient default; upper body-temperature boundary; one absolute `T_body`; battery joules; declared normalization bounds; the existing four actions. |
| **Organism-visible** | Normalized own battery energy; normalized own current body temperature; existing directional beacon values; existing `charging_contact`; the existing `WAIT`, `TURN_LEFT`, `TURN_RIGHT`, and `MOVE_FORWARD` interface. |
| **Evaluator-only** | Ambient temperature; battery joules, Wh, voltage, capacity, and SOC; absolute electrical power; charger phase, input, accepted power, efficiency, loss, and termination state; actuator electrical input; actuator body-coupled heat; thermal-source decomposition; cooling/exchange power; physical world scale; body dimensions, mass, and geometry; component temperatures; hardware provenance and measurement metadata; diagnostics and labels. |

No evaluator-only quantity may leak into a plastic update except through a
later authorized organism-visible consequence that has actually occurred.
ADR 0010 continues to govern the recursive provenance of every retained causal
object. The evaluator may record privileged state for physics and analysis,
but evaluator access does not imply organism access.

### R. Learner and controller boundary

Opening the V0.4 ecology does not authorize:

- model-guided action selection;
- planning or counterfactual control;
- larger neural learners or a learned world model;
- a new reward or reinforcement learning, PPO, or deep RL;
- a new memory architecture;
- wheel proprioception, motor-current observation, or learned charger
  control;
- a controller emergency-rescue or evaluator override.

ADR 0010 remains the governing V0.3 lifetime-plasticity boundary. Any learner
used in later V0.4 development must learn from the new V0.4 organism-visible
transitions under its separately declared and authorized mechanism. D-013 and
D-018 learner results remain historical and do not authorize transfer of old
weights or old learned state.

The preferred first implementation step is a fixed-action/schedule bookkeeping
probe and deterministic accounting tests. Whether a learner can regulate the
new ecology is a later question requiring the relevant developmental decision
and boundary compliance.

## First-slice parameter freeze

The ADR deliberately does not invent every uncertain physical constant. It
authorizes the structural choices and ranges above and requires the first V0.4
implementation/development record to declare one exact initial
parameterization **before any substantive behavioural run**.

That declaration must include at minimum:

- `dt`;
- world scale;
- movement increment;
- `E_battery_max` in joules;
- electronics electrical power and electronics body-heat approximation;
- MOVE actuator electrical power;
- TURN actuator electrical power;
- MOVE actuator body-heat estimate;
- TURN actuator body-heat estimate;
- charger accepted-power rule;
- charger body-heat/loss rule;
- taper and termination rule;
- `C_thermal`;
- `G`;
- evaluator ambient default;
- fixed own-temperature normalization bounds;
- upper-temperature viability boundary;
- initial battery state;
- initial body temperature.

Every uncertain value must retain a provenance label such as **DESIGN CHOICE**,
**DERIVED**, **MANUFACTURER REFERENCE**, **DATASHEET**, **ENGINEERING
ESTIMATE**, or **UNKNOWN / NEEDS MEASUREMENT**, with its source and confidence
where applicable. Once declared for the first substantive development slice,
these values must not be tuned against behaviour merely to obtain a desired
thermal problem. A later change requires an explicit development record and,
when it changes the durable boundary or interpretation, the appropriate ADR
review.

## Not authorized by this ADR

This ADR does not authorize:

- real physical robot control or physical-device actuation;
- wheel encoders, RPM sensing, motor-current observation, or wheel
  proprioception;
- wheel slip, wheel dynamics, detailed collision geometry, wheel collision
  geometry, or obstacle physics;
- camera vision;
- networking or external APIs;
- multiple thermal compartments or component-temperature observations;
- external-temperature sensing, ambient-relative thermal observation, or
  temperature maps;
- movement-dependent cooling in the first slice;
- detailed battery electrochemistry, voltage-versus-SOC curves, battery
  degradation, ageing, health state, or heat-dependent capacity degradation;
- detailed charger-electronics simulation;
- carrying D-002 offered-input heat unchanged into V0.4;
- treating all actuator electrical power as body heat without an explicit,
  provenance-labelled sensitivity assumption;
- controller emergency rescue, evaluator success labels, or new reward;
- a new learning mechanism, model-based control, planning, RL/PPO/deep RL,
  JEPA-scale world model, or LLM cognition;
- changes to historical D-002 through D-019 code, evidence, results, seed
  reservations, or interpretations;
- any confirmatory EXP protocol or evidence claim;
- any claim of consciousness, emotion, subjective experience, genuine life,
  metabolism, or safe physical embodiment from simulator behaviour.

## Consequences

- V0.4 can represent battery energy in joules and one lumped own-body
  temperature while preserving a minimal organism-visible interface.
- Physical energy depletion and thermal heating use distinct causal accounting
  paths, especially for actuators and charging.
- Signed exchange toward evaluator-only ambient replaces arbitrary fixed
  cooling in the new ecology and permits physically coherent warming below
  ambient.
- Full-battery contact no longer has to imply the same charging heat as active
  mid-SOC charging.
- The reference body provides scale and thermal-mass context without creating a
  permanent commercial-hardware commitment or authorizing physical control.
- Broad D-019 estimates remain uncertainty and sensitivity inputs. They are not
  measurements and cannot be silently promoted to hardware truth.
- The realistic physical model may produce little or no thermal regulation
  pressure. That null result is retained rather than engineered away.
- V0.3 plasticity remains available only under ADR 0010's provenance closure;
  V0.4 itself does not select a controller, learner, or reward.
